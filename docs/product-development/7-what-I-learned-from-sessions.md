#### Networking is important

**The Power of Networking**: The speaker repeatedly stresses that building a long-term network is the most critical resource for an entrepreneur, explaining that it directly facilitates team recruitment, partnerships, funding, and exit opportunities. For early-career founders who may feel they have little to offer, the speaker notes that their greatest asset is their time.

**Modern Startup Moats (Distribution and Data)**: The speaker asserts that software features and visual designs are easily copied overnight in the current AI landscape, meaning software itself is no longer a defensible moat. Instead, the only true remaining advantages are proprietary data and distribution edges.

### ICP scoring matrix

The ICP scoring matrix basically scores your problem and solution on several different metrics so you can see who your ICP is. 

Rate each segment on a scale of **1 to 5** for questions within four main categories, with specific weights applied to emphasize what matters most:

1. **Pain (Weight: 3x — Max: 75 points)**
    
    - Measures the urgency and intensity of the problem you solve for this segment. High scores go to segments experiencing acute, daily friction or costly problems.
        
2. **Power (Weight: 2x — Max: 50 points)**
    
    - Evaluates decision-making authority and speed. High scores go to segments where the end user has the direct budget or authority to purchase without lengthy corporate approval cycles.
        
3. **Fit (Weight: 2x — Max: 50 points)**
    
    - Measures how well your current product align with their specific requirements and workflows without needing custom, high-effort modifications.
        
4. **Economics (Weight: 1x — Max: 25 points)**
    
    - Assesses the customer lifetime value, willing price point, and cost to acquire or service the segment.

You should score these metrics from 1 to 5:

- **pain (weight: 3x)**: 5 if it is a problem that happens everyday or 1 if it is a problem that happens once a year
- **power (weight: 2x)**: Does your ICP on a scale from 1 to 5 actually have the ability to pay for the solution? Is it affordable for who your ICP is? 
	- 5 if your ICP does pay regularly for this type of solution
	- 3 if an engineer could buy it with his manager's sign og
	- 1 if your ICP has no ability to buy it, or don't want to buy it (stingy like developsrs)
- **fit (weight: 2x)**: For the solution you created, how closely does that solution fit the problem the ICP has on a scale from 1 to 5.
	- 5 if your proposed solution solves their problem really well
	- 1 if it doesn't solve their problem at all
- **economics (weight 1x)**: For your ICP, how much is it cost to get your solution in front of them and once you get it in front of them, how much do the pay.
	- 3 if you break even, where it takes $100 to get in front of them and they pay $100 for your product. 
	- 1 if it takes you $500 to get in front of an ICP and they're only willing to pay $5/month for your produce.

Here is the formula for the ICP matrix:


$$\text{Total Score} = (\text{Pain} \times 3) + (\text{Power} \times 2) + (\text{Fit} \times 2) + (\text{Economics} \times 1)$$

Customer discovery calls should be 30 to 45 minutes and be live on video. 

> [!NOTE]
> The purpose of a customer discovery call is to **validate problems**, not sell solutions

#### **before the call**

- [ ] Send calendar invite with video link
- [ ] Confirm they're in your target ICP
- [ ] Have this script open with notes ready

#### **opening of the call**

Establish that this is not a sales call, you just want to get to know their day-to-day life

> "Hey `[Name]`, thanks for making time.
> 
> Quick context: **This is NOT a sales call.** I'm not pitching anything.
> 
> I'm exploring `[one-sentence description]` and talking to `[their role]` to understand real challenges. Best thing you can do is be brutally honest — even if that means telling me my idea sucks.
> 
> Mind if I record so I can focus on the conversation?"


#### Part 1: gathering context 

- Q1: "Walk me through your typical `[day/week]` when it comes to `[problem area]`."
	- Follow-ups: "Give me a specific example from last week?" / "What tools are you using?"
- Q2: "What's the most frustrating part about how you handle `[problem]` today?"
	- Follow-ups: "Tell me about the last time that happened?" / "What was the impact?"

#### Part 2: Problem validation 

- Q3: "How are you currently solving `[problem]`?"
	- Follow-ups: "Give me a specific example from last week?" / "What tools are you using?"
- Q4: "What would happen if you didn't solve this?"
	- Follow-ups: "Are you actively working on this now?" / "What's the cost of inaction?"
- Q5: "On a scale of 1-10, how painful is this right now?"
	- If 7+: "What makes it a `[number]` and not a 3?"
	- If < 7: "What would make this a higher priority?"
- Q6: "Have you spent money trying to solve this? How much?"
	- Follow-ups: "Was it worth it?" / "What would you have paid for something that actually worked?"


#### Part 3: solution exploration

- Q7: "If you could wave a magic wand and solve this perfectly, what does that look like?"
	- Follow-ups: "What outcomes would you expect?" / "How would you measure success?"
- Q8: "What's the ONE thing that would make the biggest difference?"
	- Follow-up: "Why that over `[other thing they mentioned]`?"

#### Part 4: commitment

- Q9: "I'm building something for this. Would you be open to being an early tester?"
	- If yes: "What would you need to see to try it? Would you pay `[price]` if it delivers `[outcome]`?"
	- If hesitant: "What concerns do you have?"
- Q10: "Who else do you know who deals with this same problem?"
	- Follow-up: "Would you be open to making an intro?"
#### Part 5: closing

> "Super helpful — thank you. Quick recap of what I heard: `[top 2-3 pain points]`. Did I get that right?
> 
> I'll `[next step]` and would love to keep you in the loop. Can I reach out in `[timeframe]`?"
